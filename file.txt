# AgriConnect
## Empowering Farmers, Redefining Agricultural Procurement

You are helping me design and develop a production-oriented agricultural procurement and e-commerce platform called **AgriConnect**.

Before making any architectural, functional, database, UI, or implementation decision, understand the complete business idea below. **Do not introduce assumptions that change the core business model. If something is genuinely unclear or could affect the system design, ask me first.**

---

# 1. Core Idea

AgriConnect is a platform designed to improve transparency and bargaining power for farmers during agricultural procurement.

The central problem is information asymmetry between farmers and suppliers/middlemen.

For example:

* Actual market price of onions may be around ₹35/kg.
* A supplier may tell a farmer that the market is down and offer ₹10/kg.
* The farmer may have limited access to alternative buyers or reliable market information.
* Because of this information imbalance, the farmer may be forced to accept an unfair price.

AgriConnect addresses this by providing:

1. Market price transparency.
2. Supplier-created procurement requirements.
3. Competitive farmer bidding.
4. Quality information.
5. Smart bid ranking and allocation recommendations.
6. Separate contracts/orders for selected farmers.
7. Supplier inventory management.
8. An e-commerce marketplace where suppliers sell acquired produce directly to customers.

The overall business flow is:

**Farmer → Supplier → Customer**

The platform has two major sides:

### B2B Procurement

**Farmer → Supplier**

Farmers compete to fulfill supplier requirements through transparent bidding.

### B2C E-commerce

**Supplier → Customer**

Suppliers sell the agricultural produce they acquired from farmers through a normal e-commerce experience.

# 6. Procurement Model

The supplier creates a procurement requirement.

Example:

Supplier needs:

**10,000 kg Onion**

Requirement:

* Product: Onion
* Required quantity: 10,000 kg
* Minimum grade: B
* Delivery deadline: 5 days
* Transport responsibility: Supplier
* Partial fulfillment: Yes
* Allocation mode: Smart
* Bid deadline: 2 days

Relevant farmers receive notifications.

Farmers then submit bids.

Example:

Farmer A:

* Quantity: 4,000 kg
* Price: ₹28/kg
* Grade: A
* Delivery: 3 days
* Images: uploaded

Farmer B:

* Quantity: 3,000 kg
* Price: ₹27/kg
* Grade: B
* Delivery: 4 days

Farmer C:

* Quantity: 5,000 kg
* Price: ₹30/kg
* Grade: A
* Delivery: 2 days

# 7. Open Bidding

Currently, bidding is **open/transparent**.

Farmers can see other farmers' submitted bids.

For example:

Farmer A may see:

* Farmer B → ₹27/kg → Grade B → 3,000 kg
* Farmer C → ₹30/kg → Grade A → 5,000 kg
* Own bid → ₹28/kg → Grade A → 4,000 kg

Do not implement sealed/private bidding unless I explicitly request it later.

---

# 8. Partial Fulfillment

Partial fulfillment is supported.

A supplier may require:

**10,000 kg**

and accept:

* Farmer A → 4,000 kg
* Farmer B → 3,000 kg
* Farmer C → 3,000 kg

Total:

**10,000 kg**

Each selected farmer must receive a **separate order/contract**.

However, the supplier must see those separate contracts/orders grouped under the original procurement request.

The relationship should conceptually be:

```text
Procurement Request
        |
        +---- Farmer Contract A
        |
        +---- Farmer Contract B
        |
        +---- Farmer Contract C
```

Do NOT create one combined farmer order.

---

# 9. Smart Ranking and Allocation

The system should NOT automatically accept bids.

Instead, AgriConnect should analyze bids and provide the supplier with recommendations/rankings.

The supplier remains the final decision-maker.

The system should consider factors such as:

* Price
* Quality grade
* Quantity
* Delivery time
* Distance
* Farmer rating
* Historical reliability

An initial example weighting can be:

* Price: 30%
* Quality: 25%
* Quantity: 15%
* Delivery: 10%
* Distance: 10%
* Farmer rating/reliability: 10%

These weights should be configurable rather than hard-coded permanently.

There are two related concepts:

### Bid Ranking

Determines which individual farmers appear stronger.

### Smart Allocation

Determines which combination of farmers can best satisfy the required quantity.

For example:

Requirement:

10,000 kg

Potential recommendation:

* Farmer A → 4,000 kg
* Farmer B → 3,000 kg
* Farmer C → 3,000 kg

Total:

10,000 kg

The supplier sees the recommendation and can:

* Accept recommendation
* Manually choose farmers

The system must not silently accept bids.

The allocation engine should prioritize satisfying the required quantity while balancing price, quality, distance, delivery, and reliability.

# 10. Quality Assurance

Use a two-stage quality process.

### Stage 1 — Farmer Declaration

The farmer provides:

* Grade A/B/C
* Produce images

The supplier can inspect the images before accepting the bid.

### Stage 2 — Physical Verification

When the produce reaches the supplier, the supplier verifies the actual quality.

Example:

Expected:

Grade A

Actual:

Grade B

The supplier should be able to record the verified quality.

Possible outcomes can include:

* Accepted
* Adjusted
* Rejected
* Disputed

AI-based image quality assessment is a **future enhancement**, not the initial authority.

Eventually an AI service can analyze produce images and provide:

* Predicted grade
* Confidence
* Possible defects

But AI should initially act as decision support rather than automatically making the final commercial decision.

```text

   ↓
```

# 16. Market Price Transparency

Real market price data is important to AgriConnect.

The system should eventually integrate with reliable agricultural market-price sources such as government mandi/market datasets and eNAM/AGMARKNET-related sources.

Example:

Government market price:

₹3,200/quintal

AgriConnect displays:

₹32/kg

because:

1 quintal = 100 kg.

Farmers should be able to compare their bid with market reference prices.

Example:

```text

Your Bid: ₹30/kg
Difference: -₹2/kg
```

Market data should be normalized and cached where appropriate.

# 18. Database Architecture

Use a **polyglot persistence approach**.

Do NOT put everything into one NoSQL database.

### PostgreSQL is the source of truth for transactional data.

Use PostgreSQL for:

* Users
* Farmer profiles
* Supplier profiles
* Customer profiles
* Procurement requests
* Bids
* Accepted bids
* Contracts
* Orders
* Inventory
* Products
* Payments
* Addresses
* Delivery status
* Important business relationships

### MongoDB

Use MongoDB for flexible/document-oriented data such as:

* AI analysis results
* Event/activity documents
* Flexible notification payloads
* Bid snapshots where useful
* Analytics/event data

MongoDB must not become the authoritative source for critical payment/order/inventory transactions unless explicitly designed later.

### Redis

Use Redis for:

* Market-price caching
* Frequently accessed data
* Rate limiting
* Temporary data
* Performance optimization

Do not use Redis as the permanent source of truth.

# 24. Concurrency

The system must safely handle concurrent operations.

Important examples:

### Bid allocation

Prevent accepting more produce than the procurement requirement.

Example:

Required:

10,000 kg

Do not accidentally accept:

13,000 kg

because two selections happened simultaneously.

Use PostgreSQL transactions and appropriate locking/concurrency controls.

### Inventory

If available inventory is:

100 kg

and two customers simultaneously try to purchase:

70 kg + 50 kg,

the system must not oversell.

Use transactional/atomic inventory updates.

---

# 25. Payment Idempotency

Payment webhooks may be retried.

Therefore:

* Payment transaction IDs should be unique.
* Processing should be idempotent.
* Duplicate webhook events must not create duplicate orders or duplicate payment records.
